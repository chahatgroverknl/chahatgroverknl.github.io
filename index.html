Pavilion, concept development, spatial experience

Dzud

A loss of access: a story of interruption and continuation.

Dzud is a severe Mongolian winter in which snow, ice and frozen ground keep livestock from the pasture they depend on. The grass is still there beneath the surface, but no one can reach it. For herders it means lost animals, long searches for open ground and whole communities put at risk.

The pavilion turns that experience into a walk. It borrows from the ger, the demountable felt tent of nomadic life, as a model for living by adapting.

Site
Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia
Length
24 m, in four zones
Structure
Glulam timber ribs, 150 × 300 mm, on removable screw piles
Skin
PTFE/PVC-coated membrane over translucent polycarbonate

Mongolia is a land of vast landscapes and nomadic traditions, where life is shaped by movement, the seasons and a close relationship with the land. The ger is how that life is housed: demountable, light and adaptable, it shelters people through extreme cold while keeping them connected to the landscape. That principle of adaptation is where the pavilion begins.

  1. Access5 mWidely spaced ribs keep the ground, sky and steppe in view. The pasture is open.
  2. Loss5 mThe ribs close in and a translucent layer covers the ground. You can still see the land, but you can’t reach it.
  3. Isolation5 mA dark, compressed passage that carries the weight of the condition.
  4. Adapt9 mA protected dome like a ger. The toono brings in light and sky before the pavilion opens back to the landscape.

The architecture does not represent the snow. It represents what the snow does.

When access is lost, adaptation becomes a way forward.

Lighting object, PVC and joinery

Crossy Light

Where structure becomes light.

The brief allowed one material and one structural system. Crossy Light uses PVC pipe as both the form and the structure of a chandelier, and leaves every joint and connection exposed as part of the design.

Brief
One material, one structural system
Material
PVC pipe, joined with nuts and bolts
Built
Full scale, by hand

A repeated system, a crossed geometry. Straight PVC sections of different lengths and directions meet through the same repeated joint.

About

My approach is rooted in minimalism, with an emphasis on simplicity, clarity and function.
